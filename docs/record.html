Calls

Each call says a stock will beat or trail SPY and its sector fund by a date. It is never a buy or a sell.

    How the record is kept

    1. Each call is committed with an outside timestamp before anyone can know how it ends.
    2. A close is a new entry. Nothing already written is edited.
    3. An idea that never publishes shows only the stage it stopped at and the date. Its ticker stays private.
    4. Every call is the same $10,000 of paper money, whatever its confidence. It is scored against SPY and its sector fund; the paper book holds it against SPY. A closed call shows one rounded excess return against each, and never a price series.

      How the event feed works

      The homepage office replays a public event feed called events.v1. The same scripts that write the ledger write it, under the same checks, and it’s append-only. Each event is one JSON line, and the real feed uses the same types and fields as the sample.

      {"v":1,"id":"E-n","ts":"<UTC time>","type":"approval.stamped","actor":"sidd","role":null,"sha":"<commit>"}

      Ids run in sequence, E-1, E-2 and on. The actor is a script, one of the three seats, or me. A published row lists the event ids that led to it, so you can walk the chain backwards.

      • A published chain’s events ship in the same commit as its row: memo drafted, risk passed, decision made, my stamp, row published.
      • Dropped chains stay ticker-less forever. A veto, a no from the chair or my discard shows up only as chain.dropped, with the stage it stopped at and a date, released in the Sunday batch.
      • No ticker appears before publication, because showing that research is pending on a name would leak it.
      • Events never carry model prose, filing text, my private data or anything about the restricted list.

      Every replay animation maps to one event type, and nothing replays without a published event behind it. A veto drops a card into the No tray. My signature lands as a stamp. The only other motion is set dressing: typing hands and turning heads while the week plays, and the night sky in dark mode. Today it all plays a labeled sample week on invented placeholder tickers.     The paper book
    
      
      
      The rule, set in advance: $100,000 of paper money, $10,000 per call, bought at the first official close after the call is committed and held to its horizon. An outperform call holds the stock against SPY. An underperform call holds SPY against the stock. Confidence never changes the amount. Anyone can recompute it from the ledger and public closing prices.
      
      
      When a call closes, the record shows one number for it: the stock’s total return minus SPY’s, and minus its sector fund’s, over the same dates, rounded to 0.1%. A win needs the predicted sign against both. The record never shows a price series, daily marks or a price chart, so an open call has a status and no figure.
      
      
      Nothing in the book yet. It starts at $100,000.

      Broker paper mirror (a rehearsal of the order path, not the record)
      
      
      A paper brokerage account places each signed outperform call by script, in whole shares worth up to $10,000, and closes it at its horizon. Underperform calls aren’t placed. Paper trading doesn’t model slippage, so this shows whether the order path works, not what fills would cost. It shows a count and no return: how many calls were placed and closed by script, and how many needed a manual fix.

      Held for the leakage checks
      
      
      A planned backtest: the same desk, run once on each week after the models’ training cutoff and before day 0, using only data dated before that week’s Friday. Its calls get their own numbers (R-n) and are counted, not scored, until 30 have closed. They never count toward the 100, and I don’t sign them one by one, because I already know how those weeks ended.

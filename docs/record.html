    How the record is kept

    1. Sealed before the outcome. Each call is committed and timestamped with an outside anchor before anyone can know how it ends.
    2. Closed, never edited. A close is a new row. Nothing already written changes.
    3. Vetoes keep their tickers private. A memo that never publishes shows only the stage it stopped at and the date.

      The paper book

      The rule, set in advance: $100,000 of pretend money, $10,000 per call, bought at the first official close after the call is committed and held to its horizon. An outperform call holds the stock against SPY. An underperform call holds SPY against the stock. Confidence never changes the amount. Anyone can recompute it from the ledger and public prices.
